The context gate adds useful noun immersion over the unchanged baseline, but it also withholds substantial valid content relative to the unrestricted name exception. This is a completed research comparison, with nothing deployed. It does not establish population accuracy or native-speaker acceptance.

All 9,535 complete messages are reused TRAIN data, evaluated at the same 25% budget and two known-family states. Original baseline and unrestricted-control decisions matched exactly, including every decision field, before any new selection. Ordinary candidates, enclosing-unit masks, live source-role bindings, order and selector behavior were retained. Direct-English relations were excluded; this experiment cannot recover their missing target meanings.

Exact changed emission identities are retained in the private audit. Messages reaching 25% emitted-word depth are 175→194 versus 256 unrestricted, and 77→80 versus 100 unrestricted. Depth counts fully contained native words, not context length.

The pre-budget exception candidates cover 3,937 focuses in 3,179 messages, all absent from ordinary baseline candidates. This is genuinely additional candidate availability, although only selection and semantic review establish useful output. From the original 2,987 explicit training rows, the frozen compiler produced 26,144 patterns, 1,157 qualified contexts and 1,134 contexts with exact original-head support. The gate admitted 1,221 native focuses, withheld 2,703 without qualified context and withheld 13 whose contextual winner lacked an originally supported current head. The underlying 7,115 corpus pairs were selected through the earlier 1,480-head cohort: this remains a biased, noun-focused reuse of that corpus.

Both agents accepted all 18 sampled emissions added over baseline. One baseline emission was displaced: author marked its capitalization minor, root accepted it. Against unrestricted selection, the gate removed nine accepted emissions and two disputed/problematic emissions: ski-race→raza was material for both; musical records→registros was material for author and uncertain for root. Thus abstention reduced errors but lost valid content; it did not produce a strict whole-message improvement over unrestricted selection in this sample. Existing unrelated errors, including temporal just→Solo and emphatic just→Apenas, remain in qualified outputs. These stratified counts cannot be extrapolated to the 9,535 messages.

All review disagreements remain in the joined evidence: cases 013, 015 and 016 differ on capitalization (four emission judgments and six option judgments); case 024 differs on registros (one emission and one option). The author treats mid-sentence capitals/lost initial capitalization as minor issues; root accepts those forms. Root retains uncertainty for the musical recording sense instead of declaring a material error. `minor_form` and `minor_issue` normalize only for comparison; original labels and reasons remain unchanged. There is no majority gold.

The 24 exposed controls contain zero baseline, three qualified and seven unrestricted nonempty outputs; both agents accept all of these nonempty outputs. The gate loses six valid emissions across four control outputs compared with unrestricted selection. These controls are diagnostic, not fresh validation.

The next decision should preserve this gate as a measured conservative option, not activate it as a complete solution. It demonstrates gain beyond the original baseline while exposing a real support-versus-retention tradeoff. Missing direct-English targets, source-title protection and inherited non-noun errors remain separate gaps; neither relaxing all name guards nor adding more rejected-proof storage follows from these results. Further implementation scope belongs to a separately frozen comparison, not this closure.
