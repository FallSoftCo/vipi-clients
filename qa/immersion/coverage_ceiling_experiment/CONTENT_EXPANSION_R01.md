# Primary-backed content expansion: frozen research decision

Removing the prior-observation admission rule and packing several disjoint source units improves useful content exposure. It does **not** yet reach the 90% conversation target or qualify sustained progression. No new candidate is enabled in native/release apps.

## What was constraining the starter

`prepare_source_unit_candidate.py` intentionally selected only mappings that happened to be selected in earlier small reviews. This was a native QA subset, not a scalable language eligibility rule. The audit finds 120 Swahili, 171 Japanese and 160 Spanish units omitted solely for being unexercised, including 111/149/138 content forms. Every omitted word had retained primary lexical evidence; all Japanese units had readings. Genuine unresolved sense/scope omissions, unchanged Spanish faces and separately unavailable target concepts remain explicit.

The new TRAIN-derived candidate retains existing starter units and admits primary-attested content forms without requiring a prior selected occurrence. It preserves source guards, protected literals, exact boundaries, real unresolved exclusions and Japanese month-family withholding. It adds 111/148/138 forms, producing 218/194/181 total units with 135/130/124 declared content IDs. The additive grouping audit below corrects those inventory counts to 131/126/120 concept groups. This is lexical evidence, not a claim that every future sentence is correct.

Three arms compare starter one-unit, expanded one-unit, and expanded disjoint packing. Packing uses the same content-first/TRAIN-rank order, at most one occurrence per concept, complete source units and the sum-of-source-words budget. Densities are 10%, actual app default 25%, and 50%. The same global TRAIN concept order defines simulated top25/top50 learned-content exclusions.
